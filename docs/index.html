What the paper claims

An agent answering a question takes steps: search, look up, answer. Sometimes it fails. You can restart from scratch, or keep the first k steps and regenerate the rest. The paper tests one rule for choosing k — resume from the step with the highest token perplexity, backed up by two — and finds no advantage over restarting, anywhere.

A null result is only as good as its controls. Your job is to decide whether these controls are sound and whether the null is trustworthy, or merely underpowered.

The design that matters most

The obvious objection: maybe the uncertainty rule just picks earlier or later steps than the control, and any difference is positional rather than informational. The newest experiment kills that objection by construction.

It does not — in any of four cells. That is the central claim to scrutinise.

Why those rows are shaded

An exact two-sided sign-flip test over m non-zero blocks cannot report a p-value below 2/2^m — only two of the 2m sign assignments are maximally extreme. Tied pairs carry no sign to flip, so they do not count toward m.

Across all 102 pairs. With so few non-zero blocks, three of the four cells sit above 0.05 on their own floor. More questions would not obviously fix it: the cell with the most pairs (33) shares the highest floor with a 23-pair cell, because its extra pairs were ties.

What a useful finding looks like

Not this

"The resolution floor argument seems fine."

This

"I verified the bound by enumerating all sign assignments for m=4 and m=6; it is correct. But Appendix H uses it to argue the design could not have reached significance, which reads as absolving an underpowering problem the study does have. I would report it as a power limitation. I did not check the replication cells' floors."

State what you checked, how, what you found, and what you did not check. A named gap is far safer than one silently assumed covered. A verdict without a method is hard to act on.

Finding nothing wrong is a real result

Please do not manufacture objections. "I checked X by doing Y and it holds" is exactly as valuable as finding a flaw, and it is what lets the author state in the paper that this was independently checked. The one thing that helps nobody is a verdict with no method behind it.

When you are done

Write it up

Add a file to the repository — docs/review_emam_2026-09.md or docs/review_muna_2026-09.md — with one short entry per numbered item: what you checked, how, what you found, and what you left unchecked.

Flag blockers out loud

Anything that would change a claim in the paper is blocking. Tell the author directly as soon as you find it, not only in the file — it may change what the paper can say, and that is better known early than late.

Why this review exists. Every number is generated from audited records, every study is frozen by checksum before execution, and all four cells reproduce with zero mismatches. Those safeguards constrain arithmetic and provenance tightly — and reasoning not at all. Much of the design and analysis was produced with AI assistance and then checked largely by the same process that produced it. Self-consistency is not correctness.
